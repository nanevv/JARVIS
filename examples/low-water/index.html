A walk is measured from low water, not from the clock.

  1. −1h 30

    Meet at the dyke. Boots on, route read out loud, the turn-back time written where everyone can see it.

  2. Low water

    The far bank. The widest the flats get. Ripples, worm casts, the shells nobody sees from shore.

  3. +1h 30

    Back on the dyke. Always. The sea doesn't negotiate, so the turn-back time doesn't either.

Four rules the water enforces

Turn back on the chart.
The time is fixed before we leave. Feeling fine at the turn-back time changes nothing.
Stay in the group.
Fog on the flats takes the horizon away. Nobody wanders off for a better shell.
Wear what gets wet.
Mud is deeper in some places than others. Old boots and a change of socks.
Weather can cancel a walk.
Wind pushes water in early. If the forecast says so, we stay on the dyke.
